NRI Investing in India

You live outside India (NRI or OCI) and want to invest in India. GIFT City funds that invest in India take your money in US dollars, and in a fund that only non-residents may hold, your gains are exempt from Indian income tax. You can also invest through Indian mutual funds from an NRE or NRO account, or keep money in NRE or FCNR(B) deposits.

The routes are always shown in this order, whatever the figures.

NRE and FCNR(B) deposits are bank deposits, not mutual funds. Their interest is exempt from Indian income tax while you are a non-resident.

An Indian mutual fund deducts tax when you redeem, at the rates shown plus surcharge and 4% cess. A lower rate under a tax treaty needs a tax residency certificate from your country and Form 41. Any tax deducted beyond what you owe can be claimed back in your return.

Money in an NRO account can be sent abroad up to USD 1,000,000 a financial year. Your bank will ask for Form 145, and above ₹5,00,000 a year an accountant's certificate in Form 146.

Whether a tax treaty reduces Indian tax on your mutual fund gains depends on the treaty and your facts. Ask a tax adviser in your country of residence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are my gains on a GIFT City fund taxed in India?

Not in a fund that only non-residents may hold: your income and gains from it are exempt from Indian income tax (Schedule VI). Most GIFT City funds that invest in India are such funds; each fund's page says whether it is. Your country of residence may tax the gains under its own law.

Can I pay from my NRE or NRO account?

RBI does not say whether a payment from an NRE or NRO account to a GIFT City fund counts as money sent abroad, so your bank decides. Money in NRO can leave India only up to USD 1,000,000 a financial year.

What happens if I move back to India?

A fund open only to non-residents must be redeemed within three months after the end of the tax year in which you become resident. Moving Back to India explains what happens to each holding.

I live in the US or Canada. Can I invest?

It depends on the fund. Many do not accept US or Canada residents, and some documents say nothing; choose your country above to see each fund's rule. Your own country's tax on these funds is not covered here.

Does a tax treaty help?

It may. On their wording, the UAE and Singapore treaties may let only your country tax gains on Indian mutual fund units, but the text is not fully clear, and fund houses deduct tax unless you give a tax residency certificate and Form 41. Ask a tax adviser.
