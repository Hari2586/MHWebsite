Types of GIFT City Funds

IFSCA sorts GIFT City funds by who may buy them. Some are open to all investors; others are offered privately to accredited investors or to those investing large amounts. Each fund also says which way it invests: in India, outside India, or both.

Who Is an Accredited Investor?

An individual with gross income of at least USD 200,000 a year, or net assets of at least USD 1,000,000 of which at least USD 500,000 is financial (your home does not count), certified by a chartered accountant within the last six months. You confirm in writing that you understand the usual investor protections may not apply.

Private fund categories: Category I invests in start-ups, small businesses, social and infrastructure projects; Category III trades in complex ways, including derivatives; Category II is everything else.

Feeder Funds and Direction

Feeder Funds

Many GIFT City funds from Indian fund houses are feeder funds: they put your dollars into another fund, often the fund house's own Indian scheme (for NRIs) or an overseas fund (for residents). Costs apply at both levels, and each fund's page names the fund it invests in.

Invests in India, or Outside India

Fund houses call these “inbound” and “outbound”. IFSCA's rules don't use the words, so we say “Invests in India” and “Invests outside India”.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are most GIFT City funds private funds?

Any registered fund manager may run a private fund, and it opens once IFSCA takes its placement memorandum on record. A GIFT City mutual fund needs a retail licence and an offer document filed with IFSCA 21 working days before launch. Of the 129 funds in our list, 111 are private funds.

Can I invest less than USD 150,000 in a private fund?

Only as an accredited investor, who has no minimum, or as an employee or director of the fund manager, from USD 40,000. Everyone else needs at least USD 150,000.

Can I read a private fund's documents before I invest?

Its placement memorandum is usually not public. The fund house gives it to eligible investors; ask us and we will help you get it.

Is a GIFT City fund that invests in India the same as an Indian mutual fund?

No. It is a separate fund in US dollars, regulated by IFSCA, that usually puts your money into the fund house's Indian scheme. Its costs, its tax and who may invest differ from the Indian scheme's.

Important Information

GIFT City funds are run from GIFT IFSC, Gandhinagar, by fund managers registered with the International Financial Services Centres Authority (IFSCA). They are not mutual fund schemes regulated by SEBI. SEBI's mutual fund rules, the riskometer and SEBI SCORES do not apply to them.

Money Honey Financial Services Pvt Ltd is an AMFI-registered Mutual Fund Distributor (ARN-60930). We are not registered with IFSCA. We introduce the GIFT City funds of fund managers we have agreements with, who pay us a commission when you invest through us. Ask us and we will tell you how much.

This page is general information about GIFT City funds, not a recommendation or investment advice. It does not consider your goals, risk profile or tax position. Read a fund's offer document or placement memorandum before you invest.

Most GIFT City funds are in US dollars. Their value in rupees rises and falls with the exchange rate, as well as with the market.

Private funds are restricted schemes, offered by private placement only to accredited investors or to investors who invest at least USD 150,000. They are not available to everyone.

For a complaint about a fund, write first to its fund manager's complaints officer. If you are not satisfied, appeal within the fund house, then email IFSCA at grievance-redressal@ifsca.gov.in. For a complaint about our service, contact us.
