Why Portfolios Need a Regular Check

  1. What Investors Find Hard After They Invest

    Asked what they found hard after investing, mutual fund and ETF investors most often named returns lower than they had hoped (41%) and not knowing whether to stay invested or exit (38%). 28% found managing several investments hard, and 23% tracking how they were doing.[1]

  2. More Folios Than Investors

    Mutual fund folios rose from 9.79 crore in March 2021 to 27.39 crore in March 2026, while the number of investors rose from 2.3 crore to 6.1 crore. That is about 4.5 folios for each investor.[2][3]

  3. More Investors Now Buy Direct

    Direct plans held 45.1% of mutual fund assets in March 2026, up from 43.4% in March 2021. Among retail investors the direct share rose from 21.4% to 36.7%. Whichever plan you hold, what you hold still needs a regular look.[3]

  4. Money Left Unclaimed Keeps Growing

    Unclaimed mutual fund money came to ₹3,811 Crores at the end of March 2026, ₹1,122 Crores of it redemptions and ₹2,689 Crores income payouts (IDCW). The unclaimed payouts rose 15.7% in a year.[4]

  5. About 1 in 7 Folios Had No Nominee

    When SEBI last counted, 14.18% of mutual fund folios held in one name had no nominee. 8.09% had neither a nominee nor a choice not to nominate, and 6.09% had chosen not to.[5]

Many folios each, money left unclaimed and folios without a nominee: each is put right by looking at what you already hold.

How to Review Your Mutual Fund Portfolio

  1. Gather everything in one place. A Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) lists your mutual fund holdings across fund houses. It is issued for each month by the 15th of the next and for each half-year by the 21st, and the half-yearly one shows, for each scheme, the commission paid to your distributor in rupees.[7]
  2. Check the mix. Add up what is in equity, debt, hybrid and gold, and set it beside the mix you meant to hold. The Asset Allocation Calculator shows how far it has moved.
  3. Look for overlap. Two funds that own many of the same shares add less than they seem to; the report shows how much yours share.
  4. Compare each fund with its own benchmark, over the same period, rather than with whichever fund did well last year.
  5. Check nominees and KYC. Name nominees on every folio, and fix a KYC status that reads On-Hold or Rejected before you need to sell. Your fund house's or registrar's website shows the status.
  6. Read what has changed. A change in a scheme's category, what it invests in or its riskometer level can change whether it still fits your goal; the report lists each one.
  7. Check each goal. Is each goal's money on track, and does it still suit the time left? The goal pages work out the monthly amount again.
  8. Decide, then act. Write down what you will change and why. Before you sell or move money, check the exit load and the tax: the Capital Gains Tax Calculator works out the tax on a gain.

Portfolio Questions People Ask

How many mutual funds should I have?

There is no right number, and we do not suggest one. What matters is that each fund does a job the others do not. SEBI requires a large cap fund to keep at least 80% in large companies, and lets a flexi cap fund put its at least 65% in equity into companies of any size, so funds in different categories can own many of the same shares.[7]

Are 5 or 7 mutual funds too many?

Not by the count alone. Five funds in five different categories can be easier to follow than three that own the same shares. The report's overlap check shows how much your funds share.

How often should I review my mutual fund portfolio?

Once a year is a common rhythm, and again after a large change in your life or your goals. Looking much more often tempts you to react to short-term moves.

What is mutual fund portfolio overlap?

The share of your money that sits in the same companies through different funds. Two funds that own many of the same shares rise and fall together, so they spread your money less than two names suggest.

How can I see all my mutual funds in one place?

A Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) lists your mutual fund holdings across fund houses. It is issued for each month by the 15th of the next and for each half-year by the 21st, and the half-yearly one shows, for each scheme, the commission paid to your distributor in rupees.[7] After you sign in, you can import a CAS from CAMS, KFintech or MF Central to add funds bought elsewhere to the report.

How many nominees can I add to a folio?

From 1 Sep 2026 you can name up to 3 nominees on a mutual fund folio, and a new folio in one name needs a nomination or a signed choice not to nominate.[6]

When should I exit a mutual fund?

Look at three things: whether you need the money for the goal now, whether the fund still fits the goal (a change in its category or what it invests in can alter that), and whether your mix has moved far from what you meant. Before you sell, check the exit load and the tax. The decision is yours; speak to us if you want help.

Is the Portfolio Health report a separate service?

No. It is a feature of your account on the investor platform. It shows what you hold, and nobody contacts you about it unless you ask, through Speak to Us in the report.
