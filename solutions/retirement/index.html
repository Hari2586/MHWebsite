Why Most Indians Retire Without a Pension

  1. Life After 60 Is Getting Longer

    An Indian who reaches 60 now lives another 18.6 years on average, and a woman 19.8 years. That is a year more than in 2007-11.[2]

  2. More of India Is Over 60

    People over 60 were 5.6% of India in 1961. They were 10.1% in 2021 and are projected to be 13.1% by 2031.[3]

  3. Few Get a Pension From Work

    Of everyone aged 60 and over, 35.7% were still working, 7.3% had retired from an organised-sector job, and 5.7% were receiving a pension from that work.[1]

  4. Today's Salaried Often Have No Cover

    In 2025, 51.7% of salaried employees outside farming were not eligible for any of PF or pension, gratuity, health care or maternity benefits.[4]

Put together: more years after 60, and for most people no pension to pay for them. Yet few save for it first. Only 4% of households name retirement as their main goal for investing; 20% name their children's education.[7]

How to Build a Retirement Corpus, and Draw on It

  1. Know your number. At the calculator's numbers, the corpus needed at 60 is ₹4,61,19,865. After what your savings so far could grow to, the gap is ₹4,61,19,865.
  2. Start the SIP that fills the gap. That is ₹24,304 a month at the rate you chose: an illustration, not a promise of any return.
  3. Start early: waiting costs. Starting 5 years later, at 40, needs ₹34,493 a month for the same corpus, about 1.4 times as much.
  4. Raise it as your income rises. The Step-Up SIP Calculator shows what raising the SIP every year does.
  5. Allow for prices, health above all. General CPI inflation averaged 4.71% a year from 2015 to 2025, and health prices 5.50% a year.[6] The example assumes 6% a year to leave a margin; change it to see the effect.
  6. Lower the risk as the date nears. In the last few years before you retire, move part of the money from equity towards hybrid and debt funds, so a market fall just before you need the money does less harm.
  7. Draw on it with an SWP. After you retire, a systematic withdrawal plan (SWP) pays out a set amount each month by redeeming units. It is not a pension: what remains can rise or fall, and it lasts only as long as the money does. The SWP Calculator shows how long a corpus lasts.

Retirement Questions People Ask

How much money do I need to retire in India?

It depends on what you spend, how fast prices rise and how many years you plan for. At the calculator's numbers, the corpus needed at 60 is ₹4,61,19,865. Change the numbers above to yours; the result is an illustration at the rates chosen.

Is ₹2 Crores enough to retire at 60?

It depends on what you spend. In the worked example (₹40,000 a month today, prices rising 6% a year, 7% a year after retiring), the corpus needed at 60 is ₹4,61,19,865, and ₹2 Crores would pay the expenses until about age 70 at the same assumed rates. Someone who spends less needs less: enter your own numbers above.

How much should I have saved for retirement at 30 or 40?

The calculator answers a related question: how much to put aside each month from now. In the worked example, a SIP of ₹17,485 a month from age 30 builds the corpus by 60 at an assumed 12% a year; starting at 40 needs ₹34,493, about 2.0 times as much. Savings you already have shrink the gap: enter them above.

How can I draw a monthly amount from my mutual funds after I retire?

Through a systematic withdrawal plan (SWP): the fund house redeems units worth a set amount each month and pays it to your bank account. It is not income and not a pension: what remains invested can rise or fall, and withdrawals larger than the growth use up the corpus. The SWP Calculator shows how long a corpus lasts at a rate you choose.

Are SWP withdrawals taxed?

Each SWP withdrawal is a redemption, so the gain in it is taxed as a capital gain by the fund's tax class. Long-term gains on equity-oriented funds (units held over 12 months) are taxed at 12.5% on the part above ₹1,25,000 a year.[9] Based on income-tax rules in force on 29 Sep 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Please consult a tax professional.

What happened to retirement funds after SEBI's 2026 changes?

SEBI's scheme categories of 26 Feb 2026 no longer include Retirement Funds. A fund house may continue a Retirement Fund it already runs.[8] A continuing fund's lock-in is set in its scheme documents. The time-to-goal table above works with or without one.

Does this page cover NPS, EPF or pension policies?

No. We distribute mutual funds, SIFs, fixed deposits and bonds, so this page does not cover or compare NPS, EPF or insurers' pension policies. PFRDA's own FAQ on NPS withdrawals is listed under Read and Share.
