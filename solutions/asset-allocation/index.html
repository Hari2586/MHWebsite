Asset Allocation: What It Is and Why It Matters

Is too much of your money in one place?

77%

The average Indian household held 77% of its wealth in land and buildings, 11% in gold and only 5% in financial assets such as deposits, shares and mutual funds.

Source: RBI, Report of the Household Finance Committee (2017), from 2012 survey data.[1]

Asset allocation is how your money is split between equity, debt, gold and the rest. Each moves in its own way, so the split decides how far your savings as a whole can rise or fall.

Why Indian Savings Sit Mostly in Property and Gold

  1. Land and Buildings Hold Most of What Households Own

    In 2019, land and buildings made up 86.9% of the value of an urban household's assets. Deposits, provident funds and insurance made up 9.1%, and shares and similar holdings 0.1%. The survey leaves gold out.[2]

  2. What Households Put Aside Goes Mostly to Deposits

    Of what households put aside each month, 54% goes into bank deposits and savings accounts and 17% into life insurance. Mutual funds and ETFs take 5%, and shares 3%.[3]

  3. Mutual Funds' Share Is Rising Slowly

    Mutual funds made up 10.5% of households' financial assets in March 2026, up from 7.2% in March 2023. Bank deposits made up 34.4%.[4]

  4. A Mix Drifts When It Is Left Alone

    Put ₹100 half in gold and half in the Sensex's shares at 2015-16's average prices, and leave it alone. Gold's share stayed between 46% and 50% for eight years, then rose to 58.9% in 2025-26, as gold's price nearly doubled in two years.[5][6]

So most households start far from any mix they chose, and a mix chosen once does not stay put. Checking it once a year is the habit that keeps it.

How to Rebalance a Mutual Fund Portfolio

  1. Know your mix. At the calculator's numbers, equity is 20.0% of your money against a target of 50%. To get back to the target today: Move ₹3,00,000 Into Equity.
  2. Choose a target for your own reasons. The share in equity depends on how long the money stays invested, what it is for and how large a fall you could sit through. Rules of thumb such as “100 minus your age” are not SEBI rules, and no single figure fits everyone.
  3. Rebalance with new money first. Point new SIPs and lump sums at the part below its target. Nothing is sold, so no tax or exit load arises.
  4. Switch only what you must. Moving money from one scheme to another is a sale of units, so capital gains tax applies by the scheme's tax class, and an exit load may apply.[8] Hybrid funds are taxed by what they hold. A conservative hybrid fund's gains are taxed at your slab rate, an aggressive hybrid or equity savings fund is usually taxed as an equity fund, and balanced advantage and multi asset funds depend on each fund's holdings.[8]
  5. Pick a rule and keep to it. Some people rebalance once a year, others when the mix moves 5 percentage points from its target. Left alone, the target mix would drift to 61.2% equity in 10 years at the calculator's rates.
  6. Count everything you hold. Deposits, provident fund, PPF and gold are part of your mix even when they sit outside funds. Enter whatever you count as the rest.
  7. Or let one fund keep the mix. Hybrid, balanced advantage and multi asset funds hold several assets within SEBI's limits for their category, so you hold one fund and make no switches yourself.

Hybrid and Multi-Asset Funds: How SEBI Sets Their Mix

Some categories hold a mix inside one scheme, within limits SEBI sets for each.

Equity and debt, moved as the fund decides

Balanced Advantage Funds

A balanced advantage, or dynamic asset allocation, fund moves money between equity and debt as its managers decide, within the limits its own scheme documents set.[7]

Multi Asset Allocation Funds

A multi asset allocation fund invests in at least three asset classes, with at least 10% in each.[7]

Aggressive, conservative and equity savings

Hybrid Funds With a Set Range

SEBI sets each hybrid category's mix. A conservative hybrid fund holds 10–25% in equity, an aggressive hybrid fund 65–80%, and an equity savings fund at least 65% in equity and related instruments, with a net equity exposure of 15–40%.[7]

For the gold part of a mix

Gold and Silver Funds

Gold and silver ETFs and funds of funds track the metal's price, so their value rises and falls with it. The rule-based Gold and Silver Funds screen lists them. Gold ETFs and gold funds of funds are taxed as "other" funds. Gains on units held up to 24 months (12 months for a listed ETF) are taxed at your slab rate; after that, at 12.5%, with no ₹1,25,000 exemption.[8]

International Funds

Funds that invest mainly outside India add other markets to a mix, and the rupee's value against other currencies moves them too. The rule-based International Funds screen lists them.

Asset Allocation Questions People Ask

What is asset allocation in mutual funds?

How your money is split between asset classes, mainly equity, debt and gold, whether through separate funds or one fund that holds several. At the calculator's numbers, 20.0% of your money is in equity.

What is the 100 minus age rule?

A rule of thumb: keep 100 minus your age as the percentage in equity, so 65% at 35. It is not a SEBI or AMFI rule, and it leaves out your goals, your income and how large a fall you could bear.

Is there a right asset allocation for my age?

No single mix fits everyone of an age. Two people of 35, one saving for a home in 3 years and one for retirement in 25, need different mixes. Please speak to us for an assessment of suitability.

Is asset allocation the same as diversification?

No. Asset allocation splits money between asset classes; diversification spreads it within each, across many companies, sectors or borrowers. A broad equity fund holds many companies, but only one asset class.

How often should I rebalance?

No rule sets it. Once a year, or when the mix moves about 5 percentage points from its target, are common choices. Rebalancing more often means more switches, each with its tax and exit load.

Is rebalancing taxed?

When it means switching, yes: a switch is a sale of units. In an equity-oriented fund, gains on units held up to 12 months are taxed at 20%; after that, at 12.5% on the part above ₹1,25,000 a year.[8] Putting new money into the part below its target is not a sale. Based on income-tax rules in force on 29 Sep 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Please consult a tax professional.

What does a 70/30 asset allocation mean?

70% in equity and 30% in everything else, usually debt. Enter 70 as the target equity share in the calculator to see how far your own mix is from it.

What is a multi asset allocation fund?

One fund that holds equity, debt and at least one more asset class, such as gold, in the proportions its scheme documents set. Multi Asset Allocation Funds lists every one.
