Why a Trust Must Watch Where Its Money Sits

  1. Half the Non-Profits on NGO Darpan Are Trusts

    Of the 6,22,453 active non-profits registered on NITI Aayog's NGO Darpan portal, 3,10,065 are trusts. Registration there is self-declared, and it is not registration for tax.[2]

  2. Tax-Exempt Does Not Mean Tax-Free

    The tax liability of entities filing Form ITR-7 rose from ₹356 Crores for assessment year 2021-22 to ₹1,043 Crores for 2025-26. A trust's income is exempt only while it meets the Act's conditions.[1]

  3. No One Counts What Trusts Hold in Mutual Funds

    SEBI's figures for mutual fund holders have no line for trusts. Everyone other than individuals, NRIs and foreign portfolio investors is counted together as corporates, institutions and others, who held 37.8% of the assets in March 2026.[3]

  4. Foreign Contribution Comes With Its Own Rules

    13,520 associations received ₹55,741.51 Crores of foreign contribution in the three years to 2021-22, and 1,827 had their FCRA registration cancelled from 2018 to 2022 for breaking the Act or its rules. Foreign contribution may not be invested in mutual funds or shares.[4][5][6]

Put together: lakhs of trusts, a tax exemption that depends on following the rules, and no official count of what trusts hold in mutual funds. The rules on where a trust may keep its money come next.

How a Trust Invests: Deed, Resolution and KYC

  1. Read the trust deed. It may limit what the trustees can buy, or name the investments it allows. A private trust invests as its trust deed directs. Where the deed is silent, the Indian Trusts Act lets the trustees invest only in securities the Central Government has notified, and if someone is entitled to the trust's income for life, the investment needs that person's written consent.[8]
  2. For a registered trust, keep to Schedule XVI. A registered charitable or religious trust may keep its money only in the forms listed in Schedule XVI of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Units of any scheme of a SEBI-registered mutual fund are one of them, as are deposits with a scheduled bank and with an eligible housing finance company.[7] No item of Schedule XVI covers deposits with other non-banking finance companies.[7] Money a registered trust invests outside the list is taxed at 30%, as the trust's income for the year it is invested.[7]
  3. Keep foreign contribution apart. Foreign contribution, and income from it, may not be used for speculative investments, which the FCRA Rules, 2011 define to include mutual funds and shares. A debt-based secure investment, such as a bank deposit, is not speculative.[6]
  4. Pass a trustees' resolution. Fund houses ask a trust for a resolution of its trustees authorising the investment.[11]
  5. Complete the trust's KYC. For a trust's KYC, the fund house needs its PAN, trust deed, registration certificate if it has one, last year's balance sheet, a list of trustees, and the PAN, photograph and proof of identity and address of each trustee.[9]
  6. Match the money to when it is needed. Money the trust will spend on its objects within a year or two fits debt funds or deposits; a corpus held for many years can include equity, whose value rises and falls more.

Trust Questions People Ask

Can a charitable trust invest in mutual funds?

Yes, if it is registered under the Income-tax Act and keeps to the permitted forms. A registered charitable or religious trust may keep its money only in the forms listed in Schedule XVI of the Income-tax Act, 2025. Units of any scheme of a SEBI-registered mutual fund are one of them, as are deposits with a scheduled bank and with an eligible housing finance company.[7]

Can a charitable trust invest in equity mutual funds?

Yes. Schedule XVI covers units of any scheme of a SEBI-registered mutual fund, so equity, debt and hybrid schemes all qualify.[7] Whether equity suits the trust depends on when it needs the money and what its deed says.

Can a private or family trust invest in mutual funds?

A private trust invests as its trust deed directs. Where the deed is silent, the Indian Trusts Act lets the trustees invest only in securities the Central Government has notified, and if someone is entitled to the trust's income for life, the investment needs that person's written consent.[8] Please consult a legal professional about your trust deed.

What documents does a trust need for mutual fund KYC?

For a trust's KYC, the fund house needs its PAN, trust deed, registration certificate if it has one, last year's balance sheet, a list of trustees, and the PAN, photograph and proof of identity and address of each trustee.[9] Fund houses ask a trust for a resolution of its trustees authorising the investment.[11]

What replaced section 11(5) of the old Income-tax Act?

A trust registered or approved under the Income-tax Act, 1961 stays registered under the 2025 Act; the rule on where it may invest moved from section 11(5) to section 350 and Schedule XVI.[7][10] Based on income-tax rules in force on 29 Sep 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Please consult a tax professional.

Is there an official list of mutual funds a trust may hold?

Under the Income-tax Act, any scheme of a SEBI-registered mutual fund qualifies, so there is no list of schemes.[7] Our Trust Approved Schemes list is a shortlist we keep, not a legal list. Some states' public trust laws have rules of their own, which this page does not cover.

Can a trust that receives foreign contribution invest in mutual funds?

Foreign contribution, and income from it, may not be used for speculative investments, which the FCRA Rules, 2011 define to include mutual funds and shares. A debt-based secure investment, such as a bank deposit, is not speculative.[6]

Can a trust invest in RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds?

No. They are open to resident individuals, minors through a parent or guardian, and Hindu Undivided Families, not to trusts. See who can invest.
