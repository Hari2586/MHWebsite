ELSS Tax Saving: Old or New Tax Regime?

Does ELSS still save you tax under your regime?

76.64%

76.64% of individual tax returns for 2023-24 income were filed under the new tax regime, which gives no deduction for ELSS.

Source: Ministry of Finance, Receipt Budget 2026-27, Annex 7.[1]

ELSS cuts your tax only in the old tax regime. If the new regime costs you less, the deduction is worth nothing to you, and the 3-year lock-in saves no tax.

Why Fewer People Are Putting Money Into ELSS

  1. The Old Deduction Now Saves Far Less Tax

    The Budget estimates the tax given up each year through the old section 80C deduction, which covered ELSS, provident fund, PPF and life insurance premiums. It was ₹1,02,557.01 Crores for 2022-23 and ₹49,317.85 Crores for 2023-24, the year whose returns the figure above counts.[1][2][3]

  2. March Collections Have Fallen

    March is the last month of the tax year, when the deduction's deadline falls. ELSS funds collected ₹4,208.01 Crores in March 2023, and ₹1,599.27 Crores in March 2026.[4]

  3. More ELSS SIPs Stop Than Start

    In August 2026, 66,540 ELSS SIPs were registered and 1,15,608 were stopped before their end date; 5,701 ran to their end. The number of ELSS SIPs fell from 30,54,747 to 29,99,978 in the month.[4]

Money has left ELSS as the new regime spread. Investors took ₹5,857.78 Crores more out of ELSS funds than they put in during 2025-26. AMFI called it the category's first-ever full-year net outflow, "driven by the growing adoption of the new tax regime among investors".[4][5]

Section 123 (Formerly 80C): How the ELSS Deduction Works

  1. Work out both regimes first. At the calculator's numbers, the year's tax is ₹2,02,800 in the old regime and ₹97,500 in the new. Lower: New Regime, by ₹1,05,300.
  2. In the new regime, ELSS saves nothing. The new tax regime, which applies unless you choose the old one, allows no deduction under section 123, so ELSS saves no tax in it.[6][7] In the new regime, a resident individual whose total income is up to ₹12,00,000 pays no income tax, after relief of up to ₹60,000 under section 156. Just above that, the tax cannot be more than the income over ₹12,00,000.[6]
  3. In the old regime, the limit is shared. In the old regime, section 123 (formerly 80C) allows a deduction of up to ₹1,50,000 a year in all, shared by ELSS, provident fund, PPF, life insurance premiums, home-loan principal, children's tuition fees and the other payments listed in Schedule XV.[6] In the worked example, EPF already takes ₹60,000 of it, so ELSS can use the other ₹90,000, which takes ₹28,080 off the old regime's tax.
  4. Count every deduction before you choose. The old regime gains with each deduction you can claim, such as home-loan interest, health insurance, HRA and your own NPS contribution. In the worked example, it would cost less than the new regime only with more than ₹5,43,750 of deductions in all.
  5. You can choose again next year. If you have no business or professional income, you choose the regime in each year's return, so the choice can change from year to year. With business income, the old regime is chosen by the return's due date, and can be given up only once.[6]
  6. Count on 3 years. ELSS money cannot be taken out for 3 years from each purchase, so put in only money you will not need sooner.
  7. Invest through the year, not at the deadline. A monthly SIP from April spreads the purchases over the year. Money put in at the March deadline is bought at one day's price, and all of it stays locked in until the same date three years on.

Old Regime: ELSS Funds

An ELSS fund invests at least 80% in equity and equity-related instruments, under the Equity Linked Saving Scheme, 2005.[9] In the old regime, what you invest is deducted from your income, up to the ₹1,50,000 limit. The rule-based Tax Saving Funds (ELSS) screen lists every one with its lock-in.

New Regime: Equity Funds Without a Lock-In

In the new regime, an ELSS lock-in buys no tax saving. Flexi Cap, Large Cap, Large & Mid Cap and Index Funds also invest in equity, with no lock-in, and their gains are taxed as an ELSS fund's are.

How ELSS Gains Are Taxed

ELSS funds are equity-oriented, so gains after the 3-year lock-in are long-term, taxed at 12.5% on the part above ₹1,25,000 a year, a limit shared with your other equity-fund gains. IDCW is taxed at your slab rate.[6]

ELSS and Tax Questions People Ask

Can I claim the ELSS deduction in the new tax regime?

No. The new tax regime, which applies unless you choose the old one, allows no deduction under section 123, so ELSS saves no tax in it.[6][7] At the calculator's numbers, the lower regime is: New Regime.

Is 80C still in the law?

Not under that number. From 1 Apr 2026 the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act, and the deduction that covers ELSS is now section 123, with its list in Schedule XV. The ₹1,50,000 limit is unchanged, and it applies only in the old regime.[6][7]

Can I switch between the old and new regime every year?

If you have no business or professional income, you choose the regime in each year's return, so the choice can change from year to year. With business income, the old regime is chosen by the return's due date, and can be given up only once.[6]

Is ELSS tax-free after 3 years?

No. ELSS funds are equity-oriented, so gains after the 3-year lock-in are long-term, taxed at 12.5% on the part above ₹1,25,000 a year, a limit shared with your other equity-fund gains. IDCW is taxed at your slab rate.[6]

What is the lock-in for an ELSS SIP?

Three years for each instalment, counted from the date its units are allotted. A SIP that runs from April 2026 to March 2027 frees its first instalment in April 2029 and its last in March 2030.

Can I withdraw from ELSS before 3 years?

Each ELSS investment must stay invested for 3 years from the date its units are allotted, and cannot be transferred or pledged in that time. A SIP buys units on a new date each month, so each instalment is locked in for 3 years from its own date. If the investor dies, the nominee or heir may withdraw after 1 year.[8]

What are the drawbacks of ELSS?

Three to weigh. The deduction exists only in the old regime. The money is locked in for 3 years, whatever happens in that time. And an ELSS fund invests mostly in equity, so its value can fall, including in the year you can first redeem.

Is ELSS still worth it in the new regime?

For tax, no: it gives no deduction there. As an equity fund it works like any other, with a 3-year lock-in added. ELSS folios reached 1,70,11,131 in March 2025, and stood at 1,61,67,785 at the end of August 2026.[4] Whether equity suits your money depends on your goals and your time; please speak to us for an assessment of suitability.
