NRI Mutual Fund Investing: Rules, KYC and Tax

Can you invest in Indian mutual funds from abroad, and how is it taxed?

US$150.7 billion

India received US$150.7 billion in remittances in 2025, more than any other country. Mexico, next, received US$64.4 billion.

Source: World Bank, World Development Indicators, personal remittances received, 2025 (updated 13 Jul 2026).[1]

Money earned abroad can be invested in Indian mutual funds from an NRE or NRO account. What you can buy, and where the money goes when you sell, depends on the account and on the country you live in.

What NRIs Send Home, and the Rules That Come With It

  1. Money Sent Home Has More Than Doubled

    Remittances to India rose from US$68.9 billion in 2015 to US$150.7 billion in 2025, 2.2 times as much.[1]

  2. Most NRI Deposits Can Go Back Abroad

    NRIs held US$165.7 billion in Indian bank deposits at the end of March 2026. Money in NRE and FCNR(B) accounts can go back abroad in full; from an NRO account, up to USD 1 million a year.[3][4]

  3. Many Fund Houses Limit US and Canada Residents

    Of the 48 fund houses we offer, 17 say in their Statement of Additional Information (SAI) that they do not accept new investments from residents of Canada, and 8 say the same of US persons. Another 12 accept US persons only on conditions, usually a paper application made while in India. The other SAIs are silent or unclear, so check each scheme's own documents.[5]

  4. Tax Is Deducted Before You Are Paid

    When an NRI redeems, the fund house deducts tax from the gain before paying out, at rates set by the type of fund and how long it was held, plus surcharge and cess. A tax treaty may lower it for someone with a tax residency certificate.[6][7]

Yet for all the money sent home, NRIs hold a small share of Indian mutual funds. NRIs and overseas corporate bodies held 3.9% of India's mutual fund assets in March 2026, ₹2,88,189 Crores.[8]

How NRIs Invest: Accounts, KYC and Payment

  1. Check your country first. Fund houses set their own terms for residents of some countries, the USA and Canada above all. The check above shows what we can offer you online.
  2. Choose the account. NRIs can invest in Indian mutual funds without special permission from RBI, paying from an NRE or NRO account or with money sent from abroad through a bank. Money invested from an NRE account or sent from abroad can go back abroad when you redeem; money invested from an NRO account comes back to it.[9][10]
  3. Complete your KYC. NRIs need a PAN and KYC with a copy of their passport (or PIO or OCI card) and proof of their overseas address, and each application carries a FATCA and CRS declaration of where they are resident for tax.[11]
  4. Invest online, or on paper in India. Through us you invest online in the schemes open to your country. Some fund houses that limit US persons accept a paper application made while you are in India.[5]
  5. Know the tax before you redeem. The fund house deducts tax from your gain when you redeem, at the rates in the check above. Where India has a tax treaty with your country, the lower of the treaty and the Act applies. To claim it you need a tax residency certificate from that country and Form 41. You can claim back tax deducted beyond what you owe by filing an Indian income tax return.[6]
  6. Keep your status up to date. If you move abroad, or come back to India, tell each fund house, so that your tax status, bank account and address in its records match where you live.

NRI Questions People Ask

Can NRIs invest in mutual funds in India?

Yes. NRIs can invest in Indian mutual funds without special permission from RBI, paying from an NRE or NRO account or with money sent from abroad through a bank. Money invested from an NRE account or sent from abroad can go back abroad when you redeem; money invested from an NRO account comes back to it.[9][10]

Can NRIs living in the USA or Canada invest in Indian mutual funds?

Some fund houses accept them, often only on a paper application made while in India, and many do not. Of the 48 fund houses we offer, 17 say in their SAI that they do not accept residents of Canada, 8 that they do not accept US persons, and 12 that they accept US persons only on conditions.[5] Choose your country in the check above to see what we can offer you online.

Should I invest from an NRE or an NRO account?

It depends on where the money comes from and where it needs to go. Money invested from an NRE account, or sent from abroad, can go back abroad when you redeem; money invested from an NRO account comes back to it.[9] From an NRO account an NRI may send up to USD 1 million abroad in a financial year, with the tax forms the bank asks for.[4]

How much tax is deducted when an NRI redeems?

Tax deducted on an NRI's gains is 20% on equity funds held 12 months or less; 12.5% on equity funds held longer, on gains above ₹1,25,000 in a year; 12.5% on other funds held over 24 months; and 30% on debt funds and on other funds held for shorter, plus surcharge and 4% cess.[6][7] Based on income-tax rules in force on 29 Sep 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Please consult a tax professional.

Can I claim back tax deducted as an NRI?

Where India has a tax treaty with your country, the lower of the treaty and the Act applies. To claim it you need a tax residency certificate from that country and Form 41. You can claim back tax deducted beyond what you owe by filing an Indian income tax return.[6] Form 121, the declaration that lets a resident with no tax avoid deduction, is not open to non-residents. A non-resident can ask the tax officer for a lower deduction certificate in Form 128.[6] Based on income-tax rules in force on 29 Sep 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Please consult a tax professional.

Do NRIs need a demat account to invest in mutual funds?

No. Units can be held in a folio, an account with the fund house, without a demat account. A demat account is needed only to hold units in demat form.

Can NRIs invest in RBI Bonds?

No. RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds are for residents of India only. Fixed deposits are open to NRIs: bank deposits from NRE, NRO or FCNR accounts, and company deposits from an NRO account only.
