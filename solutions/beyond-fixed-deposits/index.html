Beyond Fixed Deposits: Alternatives to FDs

Is most of your saving in FDs?

61.8%

In March 2026, 61.8% of bank term deposits earned less than 7% a year, up from 27.3% a year earlier.

Source: RBI, Annual BSR-2 on Deposits, March 2026.[1]

A deposit that falls due is renewed at the rate of the day, and its interest is taxed every year at your slab rate. Spreading money across options that differ in tax, access and risk means no single rate or tax rule decides all of it.

Where Saving Goes, and What Deposits Keep

  1. Deposits Take the Largest Share of New Saving

    Of the money households put into financial assets in 2025-26, 34.4% went into bank deposits, the largest share. Provident and pension funds took 20.9%, life insurance 13.0% and mutual funds 12.7%.[5]

  2. Deposit Rates Follow the RBI's Repo Rate

    Banks' average rate on new term deposits was 3.75% in August 2021, when the RBI's repo rate was 4.00%. It rose to 6.49% by August 2024, with the repo rate at 6.50%, and was 5.67% in August 2026, after cuts to 5.25%. The RBI raised the repo rate to 5.50% on 7 October 2026. A deposit renewed at maturity gets the rate of that day.[2][6][7]

  3. Tax Takes Its Share Every Year

    FD interest is taxed at your slab rate, plus 4% cess, every year as it accrues, even on a deposit that pays out only at maturity. Of every ₹1,000 of interest, you keep ₹792 at a 20% slab and ₹688 at 30%.[3][8]

  4. Deposit Insurance Covers Most Accounts, Not Most Money

    DICGC's cover of ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank fully covered 97.6% of bank accounts in March 2025, but 41.5% of the money in them. The part of one depositor's money at one bank above ₹5,00,000 is not insured.[9][10]

None of this makes a deposit the wrong place for money you need soon. It means one product is doing every job, including jobs that are years away.

How to Spread Money Beyond Fixed Deposits

  1. Know what your deposit keeps. At the calculator's numbers, ₹10,00,000 at 6.56% pays ₹65,600 of interest in the first year before tax, and ₹51,955 after it. With prices rising 5% a year, the deposit gains +0.19% a year in what it can buy. After 5 years it is worth ₹10,09,345 in today's money.
  2. Sort the money by when you need it. Money for the next year, money for the next few years and money for goals more than five years away can sit in different places. The first must be reachable within days; the last has time to ride out falls in value.
  3. Keep your emergency money where you can reach it. A savings account, a bank FD that can be closed early, or a liquid fund. The Emergency Fund page works out how much.
  4. Compare the facts, not only the rate. Interest is taxed every year as it accrues. A debt fund's gains are taxed at the same slab rate, in the year you redeem. Lock-ins, early-exit costs, deposit insurance and credit ratings differ too: the table below sets them side by side.
  5. Spread large deposits. DICGC insures each depositor in a bank up to ₹5,00,000, principal and interest together, and deposits at different banks are insured separately.[10] Company FDs are not insured by DICGC: they depend on the company's ability to repay, and their rating can change.
  6. Stagger the maturities. Splitting one large deposit into several that mature in different years means not all of it is renewed at one time, at one rate, and some of it falls due every year.
  7. Give long-term money a mix. For goals five or more years away, some people hold part of their money in equity or hybrid funds, whose value can fall sharply as well as rise. How much is a choice about your goals and how large a fall you could sit through. The Asset Allocation page shows how to set a mix and keep to it.
  8. Move in steps, if you move. A Systematic Transfer Plan moves a fixed sum each month from a debt fund into an equity fund, so the money goes in over months, not on one day. Each transfer is a redemption, taxed as one. The STP Calculator illustrates one.

Questions People Ask About Alternatives to FDs

Is FD interest taxable every year, even if I don't withdraw it?

Yes. Interest on a cumulative FD accrues each year, so it is taxed each year at your slab rate, and tax is deducted each year above the threshold, even though you are paid at maturity. Banks deduct tax at 10% once their interest to you passes ₹50,000 in a financial year (₹1,00,000 for senior citizens); companies, once it passes ₹10,000.[3]

Is FD interest taxed under the new tax regime?

Yes, at your slab rate, like other income from other sources. The new regime's relief that makes tax nil on total income up to ₹12,00,000 can cover it for smaller incomes. Senior citizens can deduct up to ₹50,000 of interest from banks, co-operative banks and the post office, in the old tax regime only.[3]

What is the DICGC insurance limit?

DICGC insures each depositor in a bank up to ₹5,00,000, principal and interest together, and deposits at different banks are insured separately.[10] It covers principal and interest together, across all your deposits at one bank in the same right and capacity. Company FDs, mutual funds, bonds and NCDs are not insured by DICGC.

How is a debt fund taxed?

Debt funds are specified mutual funds under the Income-tax Act, 2025, so gains on units bought from 1 Apr 2023 are taxed at your slab rate, however long you hold them.[3] Tax is due in the year you redeem, not each year as the value grows. If you choose the IDCW option, each payout is taxed at your slab rate, with 10% deducted above ₹10,000 a year from one fund house.

How do RBI Bonds differ from a bank FD?

RBI Bonds are issued by the Government of India. Their rate is reset every six months to the NSC rate plus 0.35%, so it can go down; interest is paid every six months, with no cumulative option. The money is locked in for 7 years, with early encashment only from age 60, and the bonds cannot be sold.[12] A bank FD has a rate fixed for its term, can be closed early at a lower rate, and is insured by DICGC up to ₹5,00,000.

What is an FD ladder?

Several deposits that mature in different years, instead of one large one. Part of the money falls due each year, so you can reach it, and only that part is renewed at the rate of the day.

Can I lose money in a debt fund?

Yes. A debt fund's value falls when interest rates rise, more so in funds holding longer bonds, and it can fall if a borrower defaults or its rating is cut. Debt funds are not bank deposits and are not insured by DICGC. SEBI requires every debt scheme to show its Potential Risk Class, a grid of the most interest-rate risk and the most credit risk the scheme can take.[13]

How much of my money should be in FDs?

No rule sets it. Money you may need within a year or two, and your emergency fund, are often kept in deposits or short-term debt; money for goals five or more years away has time to ride out falls in value. Please speak to us for an assessment of suitability.
