Capital Gain Bonds

Section 85 (earlier 54EC) Capital Gain Tax Exemption Bonds

See current bonds (3)

When you sell land or a building you have owned for more than 24 months, the profit is a long-term capital gain, and it is taxed. Capital Gain Bonds let you avoid that tax: invest the gain in these bonds within six months of the sale, keep them for five years, and the gain you invested is not taxed. The bonds are issued by three Government of India-owned companies, pay a fixed rate of interest once a year, and return your money at the end of five years.

Rules and limits as on 29 Sep 2026. Section 85 of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

How Capital Gain Bonds Work

  1. 1

    Sell Land or a Building

    Work out the long-term capital gain: the sale price minus the cost and the expenses of the sale. That is the amount you can protect from tax.

  2. 2

    Apply Within Six Months

    Fill in the issuer's form and pay by cheque or RTGS. Allotment is on the last day of the month the money arrives.

  3. 3

    Receive Interest Each Year

    Interest is paid on the issuer's fixed date each year and is taxable. The bonds cannot be sold or pledged.

  4. 4

    Get Your Money Back After Five Years

    The bonds are repaid at face value five years after allotment, with the last part-year of interest.

Example

You sell a flat on 20 Aug 2026 with a long-term gain of ₹30,00,000 and invest it in REC's Capital Gain Bonds; REC receives the money on 15 Oct 2026. The ₹30,00,000 gain is not taxed, which saves ₹3,90,000 of tax at 12.5% plus 4% cess (before any surcharge). The bonds pay ₹1,11,329 of interest on 30 Jun 2027, then ₹1,57,500 each year, ₹7,94,404 in all before tax, and the ₹30,00,000 is repaid on 31 Oct 2031. Words You Will See on a Bond Page

Series
One year's issue by a company, open from 1 April to 31 March. REC's Series XX is its 2026-27 issue.
Coupon
The yearly interest rate on the ₹10,000 face value of each bond. It is fixed for the five years.
Deemed Date of Allotment
The last day of the month in which the issuer receives your money. The five years run from this date.
Physical or Demat
You can hold the bonds as a certificate or in a demat account. A demat account is optional.
Private Placement
The bonds are issued under an Information Memorandum, not a public prospectus, and are not listed.
Credit Rating
An agency's opinion of the issuer's ability to pay on time. AAA is the highest rating on the scale.

Who Can Invest

Any taxpayer with a long-term gain from land or a building: individuals, HUFs, firms, LLPs, companies and trusts. Non-resident Indians can invest from an NRO account on a non-repatriable basis, and tax is deducted at source on their interest. You need a PAN and a bank account; a demat account is optional.

Understand the Risks

  • Locked in. Five years with no way out: the bonds cannot be sold, transferred or pledged, and a loan against them makes the gain taxable.
  • Credit risk. The issuers are rated AAA, but the bonds are not guaranteed by the Government of India, and ratings can change.
  • Tax conditions. The exemption depends on meeting every condition of Section 85. The interest is taxable, and tax laws can change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are 54EC bonds and Capital Gain Bonds the same?

Yes. The Income-tax Act, 2025 moved Section 54EC to Section 85 from 1 April 2026, with the same conditions. The issuers use both names.

Can I invest the whole sale price?

You can invest up to ₹50,00,000, but only the capital gain is exempt. Money invested above the gain earns interest but saves no extra tax.

Can I get my money back before five years?

No. The bonds cannot be redeemed early, sold, transferred or pledged. Plan to leave the money for the full five years.

Is the interest tax-free?

No. The interest is taxable at your slab rate. The issuers do not deduct tax at source for resident investors, so declare it in your return.

These are tax-specific bonds, meant for investors claiming the Section 85 exemption on long-term capital gains from land or buildings, subject to its conditions.

Capital Gain Bonds are not bank deposits, are not insured by DICGC and are not guaranteed by the Government of India. They carry the issuer's credit risk, and ratings can change. They are locked in for 5 years, cannot be transferred, pledged or sold, and are not listed. The interest is taxable. The exemption depends on meeting the conditions of Section 85, and tax laws can change. The issuer may revise the coupon or close the issue early. Consult a tax adviser.
