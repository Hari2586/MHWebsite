Investor Categories and How to Apply

An NCD public issue is open for a few days. You apply through UPI or ASBA, the money stays blocked in your bank account until allotment, and the NCDs are credited to your demat account and listed within three working days of the issue closing.

Rules and limits as on 29 Sep 2026.

Investor Categories

Each prospectus sets its own investor categories and how much of the issue is reserved for each. Most issues use four categories like the ones below. Since 21 January 2026, SEBI defines a retail individual investor in an NCD issue as one applying for no more than ₹2,00,000, and recent prospectuses use that amount to separate retail and high net worth investors.

CategoryWho Is Usually Included
I: InstitutionalBanks, financial institutions, mutual funds, insurance companies, provident, pension and gratuity funds
II: Non-institutionalCompanies, trusts, LLPs, partnership firms, co-operative societies and other bodies, for any amount
III: High net worth individualsResident individuals and HUFs applying for more than ₹2,00,000 across all series
IV: Retail individual investorsResident individuals and HUFs applying for up to ₹2,00,000 across all series

Non-resident Indians, foreign portfolio investors and other foreign investors are usually not eligible. Check the "Who can apply" section of each prospectus.

Additional Interest for Some Investors

Since 21 January 2026, an issuer may offer additional interest, or a lower issue price, to senior citizens, women, serving or retired defence personnel and their widows or widowers, and retail individual investors. The benefit is for the original allottee only and is lost if the NCD is transferred. The prospectus states who qualifies and how much.

Ways to Apply

MethodHow It WorksLimit
UPIYou enter your UPI ID in the application and approve a mandate in your UPI app. The amount is blocked in your bank account until allotment.Up to ₹5,00,000 per application. UPI is mandatory for individual investors applying through an intermediary up to this amount.
ASBAYour bank (a self-certified syndicate bank) blocks the amount in your account, through net banking or a form at the branch.No upper limit

You can also apply through your stockbroker or the exchange's own application platform. You may submit up to five applications from one ASBA bank account.

Steps to Apply

  1. Make sure you have an active demat account and a bank account in your own name.
  2. Read the prospectus, especially the risk factors, the security and the terms of each series.
  3. Check which investor category you fall in, based on the total amount you will apply for across all series.
  4. Choose one or more series to match your time horizon and whether you want regular interest.
  5. Enter your DP ID, client ID, PAN and UPI ID or bank account correctly.
  6. Keep enough money in the bank account for the amount to be blocked.
  7. Apply before the issue closes, and if you used UPI, approve the mandate by 5:00 pm on the working day after the issue closes.

Allotment and Listing Timeline

For issues opening on or after 1 November 2025, SEBI requires listing within three working days of the issue closing (T+3).

DayWhat Happens
T (issue closes)Bidding and changes to bids end at 5:00 pm. Banks confirm the amounts blocked.
T+1Rejected bids are removed, and the basis of allotment is prepared and approved by the stock exchange.
T+2Money moves to the issuer before 2:00 pm, allotment is made and filed before 2:45 pm, NCDs are credited to demat accounts, and blocked amounts for unallotted bids are released.
T+3The NCDs are listed and trading starts on the exchange.

If the issue is oversubscribed, NCDs are allotted first-come, first-served by the date of upload on the exchange platform, until the day the issue is fully subscribed. Bids received on that day and after are allotted proportionately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I withdraw my NCD application?

Yes, before the issue closes. After it closes, you can ask the registrar to withdraw it before the basis of allotment is finalised.

What happens to my money if I am not allotted NCDs?

The amount blocked in your bank account is released, normally on T+2. If the release is late, SEBI's investor charter entitles you to interest at 15% a year for the delay.

Which category am I in if I apply for ₹3,00,000?

In most 2026 issues, an individual applying for more than ₹2,00,000 across all series is in the high net worth category (III). Check the prospectus of the issue.

Investments in debt securities, municipal debt securities/ securitised debt instruments are subject to risks including delay and/ or default in payment. Read all the offer related documents carefully.

NCDs are not bank deposits and are not insured by DICGC. This guide is general information, not investment or tax advice.
