NCD IPO

Non Convertible Debentures - Public Issues

See current issues (3)

An NCD (non-convertible debenture) is a way to lend money to a company. You invest an amount for a fixed term, the company pays interest at a rate it states in advance, and it repays your money at the end of the term. Finance companies, such as housing, gold loan and infrastructure lenders, raise money this way through public issues that stay open for a few days. Unlike a share, an NCD cannot be turned into the company's shares; unlike a bank deposit, it is not insured by DICGC.

Rules and limits as on 29 Sep 2026.

How an NCD Works

  1. 1

    Apply While the Issue Is Open

    Choose one or more series and apply through UPI or your bank (ASBA). The money stays in your account, blocked, until the NCDs are allotted.

  2. 2

    Get NCDs in Your Demat Account

    Within 2 working days of the issue closing, the NCDs are credited to your demat account. They are listed on the exchange on the third.

  3. 3

    Receive Interest

    The company pays interest into your bank account on the dates set for your series, or all at maturity for a cumulative series.

  4. 4

    Get Your Money Back

    At maturity the company repays the face value. Before that, you can sell on the exchange at the market price, which may be above or below what you paid.

Example

₹9,500 is paid each year on ₹1,00,000 in a series paying 9.5% a year, yearly, for 3 years, and the ₹1,00,000 comes back with the last payment: ₹1,28,500 received in all, before tax. The same amount in a cumulative series at 9.5% pays nothing until maturity and then ₹1,31,293. Try your own figures in the NCD calculator.

Words You Will See on an Issue Page

Series
One option within an issue, with its own tenure, interest rate and payout. An issue usually has 6 to 12.
Coupon Rate
The yearly interest rate on the face value of the NCD.
Effective Yield
The yearly return if held to maturity, allowing for how often interest is paid. Use it to compare series; issue pages show the highest as "Returns up to".
Credit Rating
An agency's opinion of the company's ability to pay on time, from AAA (the highest) down to D (in default).
Secured or Unsecured
Whether the NCD is backed by a charge on the company's assets. Secured holders are repaid first.
Cumulative
Interest is not paid out along the way but added, and paid with the principal at maturity.
Pre-book
Applications taken before the issue opens; they are processed on the opening day.
Investor Category
Issues reserve parts for categories I to IV. Individuals applying for up to ₹2,00,000 are usually category IV, retail.

Who Can Invest

Resident individuals and HUFs, companies, trusts and institutions can apply, each in its own investor category. Individuals applying for up to ₹2,00,000 across all series are usually retail investors. You need a PAN, a demat account and a bank account in your own name. Non-resident Indians are usually not eligible; each prospectus says who can apply.

Understand the Risks

  • Credit risk. The company may delay or fail to pay interest or principal. The credit rating is the first thing to check.
  • Interest rate risk. If interest rates rise, the NCD's market price falls. This matters if you sell before maturity.
  • Liquidity risk. Many NCDs trade rarely, so selling early can be slow or at a lower price.

Frequently Asked Questions

What can go wrong with an NCD?

The company may delay or fail to pay interest or principal, and if you sell before maturity the price may be lower than you paid. A secured NCD and a higher rating lower the credit risk but do not remove it.

How much do I need to start?

Usually ₹10,000, which is 10 NCDs of ₹1,000 face value, and then in multiples of ₹1,000. Each issue sets its own minimum.

Can I get my money back before maturity?

Only by selling the NCDs on the exchange at the market price, or through a put option if your series has one. The company does not buy them back on request.

How is NCD interest taxed?

Interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. TDS of 10% is deducted from interest on listed NCDs above ₹10,000 a year.

Investments in debt securities, municipal debt securities/ securitised debt instruments are subject to risks including delay and/ or default in payment. Read all the offer related documents carefully.

NCDs are not bank deposits and are not insured by DICGC.
