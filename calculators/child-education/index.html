Child Education Calculator

Education costs are known today but paid years later. This calculator raises today's cost by the inflation rate you enter, takes off what existing savings could grow to, and works out a monthly SIP for the gap.

Example Course

A 4-year professional degree at a private unaided college cost about ₹3,72,000 at 2025 prices (NSS Report No. 585, 2017-18 costs raised by MoSPI's CPI for education), rounded up. Tuition and other fees rose 4.91% a year in the CPI from 2015 to 2025.

Not a return, so the 2% to 13% limit does not apply. MoSPI's CPI for education rose 4.61% a year from 2015 to 2025.

Asset Class

Assumed 12% a year. For reference, the mean 10-year rolling return of the Nifty 50, 1 Jun 2013 to 30 May 2023, was 12.93% (AMFI).

How the Child Education Calculator Works

The same three steps as the Goal SIP calculator, with a default education inflation rate of 5% a year:

Cost then = C × (1 + inflation)t
Gap = Cost then − S × (1 + r)t
SIP = Gap × i ÷ [((1 + i)n − 1) × (1 + i)]

where C is today's cost, S existing savings, r the yearly rate, i the monthly rate and n the months until the money is needed.

Worked Example

A course costing ₹4,00,000 today, needed in 12 years, with fees rising 5% a year: ₹4,00,000 × (1.05)12 = ₹7,18,343. With nothing saved yet, the illustrated SIP at 12% a year is ₹2,229 a month. Saving ₹1,00,000 today at the same rate would grow to ₹3,89,598 and cut the SIP to ₹1,020. The High-Cost Course example, ₹15,00,000 rising 8% a year, comes to ₹37,77,255 and an SIP of ₹11,721.

Why Real Results Differ

  • Fees for a particular course or country can rise much faster, or slower, than the rate entered; overseas costs also move with the rupee.
  • Markets do not grow at a steady rate: returns change every year and can be negative, and the order of good and bad years changes the outcome.
  • Money needed on a fixed date is exposed to the markets in the last few years; many parents move it to steadier assets as the date nears.
  • Tax on gains, exit loads and stamp duty reduce what you receive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why 5% inflation?

MoSPI's Consumer Price Index for tuition and other fees rose 4.91% a year from 2015 to 2025, and for education as a whole 4.61%. Fees at sought-after private or overseas institutions may rise faster, but no official series measures them, so the High-Cost Course example uses 8% as an assumption. Enter the rate you think fits the course.

Where does the ₹4,00,000 come from?

NSS Report No. 585 (2017-18) put a year of engineering at a private unaided college at ₹69,155. Four years, raised to 2025 prices with MoSPI's CPI for education, come to about ₹3,72,000, rounded up here. Your course may cost far more or less.

What are Children's Funds?

A SEBI solution-oriented category with a lock-in of at least 5 years or until the child comes of age, whichever is earlier.

Should the SIP stop when the child starts college?

The illustration assumes the full amount is needed on one date. Costs spread over several years can be handled with an SWP.
