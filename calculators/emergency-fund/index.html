Emergency Fund Calculator

An emergency fund pays essential bills if income stops or a large cost arrives without warning. This calculator works out the fund for the months of expenses you choose, raised by inflation to the date it is complete, what your savings already cover, and the monthly amount that could fill the gap at a steady rate you choose.

Rent, food, bills, EMIs, insurance and school fees: what you could not stop paying.

Six months is a common choice, not a rule; people with irregular income often keep more.

Not a return, so the 2% to 13% limit does not apply. India's CPI inflation has mostly been between 4% and 7% a year in the last decade.

Asset Class

Assumed 7% a year. For reference, the mean 10-year rolling return of the 10-year G-Sec, 1 Jun 2013 to 30 May 2023, was 7.20% (AMFI).

How the Emergency Fund Calculator Works

The fund is your monthly expenses times the months of cover, raised by inflation to the month it is complete. Savings you already have are assumed to grow at the rate you choose, and a monthly amount fills the rest:

Fund then = E × M × (1 + inflation)t ÷ 12
Gap = Fund then − S × (1 + r)t ÷ 12
Monthly amount = Gap × i ÷ [((1 + i)t − 1) × (1 + i)]

where E is monthly expenses, M the months of cover, S your savings today, t the months to build it, r the yearly rate and i the monthly rate.

Worked Example

Monthly expenses of ₹40,000 and 6 months of cover make ₹2,40,000 today. Built over 1 year, the fund has to keep up with prices: at 5% a year that is ₹2,40,000 × (1.05)1 = ₹2,52,000. With nothing set aside yet and the money illustrated at 7% a year, the monthly amount is ₹20,217. Building it in half the time, 6 months, takes ₹40,160 a month.

Why Real Results Differ

  • Debt and liquid fund returns vary too, and can be lower than the rate entered.
  • Redemptions must be paid within 3 working days under SEBI's rules, and often arrive sooner; only some liquid and overnight schemes offer instant redemption, up to ₹50,000 a day.
  • Your essential costs can rise faster or slower than the inflation rate entered.
  • Tax on gains, exit loads and stamp duty reduce what you receive.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many months of expenses should an emergency fund hold?

There is no fixed rule. Six months is a common choice; people whose income is irregular, or who support others, often keep more. The calculator takes 1 to 24 months.

Where do people keep an emergency fund?

Often partly in a bank savings account and partly in liquid or overnight funds. Liquid and overnight funds are not bank deposits: their value can fall, and redemptions must be paid within 3 working days, often sooner.

What is instant redemption?

Some liquid and overnight schemes pay out up to ₹50,000 a day, or 90% of the holding's value if that is less, on the same day. The Instant Redemption Funds screen lists the schemes whose own documents say they offer it.
