Mutual Fund Capital Gains Tax Calculator

Mutual fund gains are taxed when units are redeemed or switched. The rate depends on the scheme's tax class and how long the units were held. Enter one purchase and one sale to see the type of gain and the tax before surcharge.

The ₹1,25,000 exemption is for all equity-oriented long-term gains in a financial year together.

How the Mutual Fund Capital Gains Tax Calculator Works

The calculator applies these rules for sales on or after 23 Jul 2024:

Tax ClassLong-Term AfterShort-Term GainsLong-Term Gains
Equity-oriented (65% or more in Indian equity)12 months20%12.5% above ₹1,25,000 a year
Specified (more than 65% in debt), bought on or after 1 Apr 2023NeverSlab rateSlab rate
Specified, bought before 1 Apr 202324 months (12 if listed)Slab rate12.5%, no exemption
Other (for example gold, international and many hybrid schemes)24 months (12 if listed)Slab rate12.5%, no exemption

Units are long-term when held for more than the period shown. The scheme pages on this site show each scheme's tax class. Surcharge (capped at 15% on these gains) and 4% cess are added to the tax; the working shows cess but not surcharge, which depends on total income.

Worked Example

Units of an equity-oriented scheme bought on 15 Apr 2024 for ₹1,00,000 and sold on 15 Sep 2026 for ₹3,00,000. They were held for 2 Years 5 Months, more than 12 months, so the ₹2,00,000 gain is long-term. The first ₹1,25,000 of equity long-term gains in the year is exempt, leaving ₹75,000 taxed at 12.5% = ₹9,375, plus 4% cess. Sold within 12 months, the whole gain would have been short-term at 20%: ₹40,000.

What the Result Leaves Out

  • Surcharge depends on your total income and is not included.
  • Units bought on different dates, as in a SIP, each have their own holding period; redemptions use the oldest units first.
  • Equity units bought before 1 Feb 2018 use the higher of cost and the 31 Jan 2018 value as the cost; enter that figure as the purchase cost.
  • Losses, set-offs and the basic exemption limit can change the tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which units are redeemed first?

The oldest ones: redemptions follow first-in, first-out. For a SIP, each instalment's gain and holding period are worked out separately.

Is a switch between schemes taxed?

Yes. A switch is a redemption from one scheme and a purchase in another, so gains on the redeemed units are taxed.

How do I find a scheme's tax class?

The scheme pages and the Explore Funds page on this site show it, based on the scheme's portfolio and documents.

Is IDCW taxed the same way?

No. IDCW is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate, with TDS of 10% above ₹10,000 a year.
