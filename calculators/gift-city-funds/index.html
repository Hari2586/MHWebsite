How the GIFT City Funds Calculator Works

Every route grows at the return you choose less its own yearly cost, for whole years:

Value before tax = amount × (1 + return − cost)years

Money in US dollars is changed at today's rate going in and at the end rate coming out; the end rate is today's rate changed by the yearly figure you enter. An Indian international fund's NAV is in rupees but follows foreign markets, so it moves with the exchange rate too. A GIFT City fund that invests in India holds rupee assets, so its dollar value moves the other way.

Tax is worked out on the gain in rupees: the value at the end, at the end rate, less the amount invested at today's rate. A gain is long-term after more than 24 months (more than 12 for an Indian equity-oriented fund). Then, route by route:

  • A GIFT City fund that also takes residents pays tax on its own gains, 14.95% long-term and 42.744% short-term (12.5% or 30%, with surcharge of 15% or 37% and 4% cess, as its offer document sets out), and its NAV is after that tax. With Plus Tax on Your Exit, your own gain is taxed again at 12.5% long-term or your slab rate short-term, plus cess; for an NRI, 12.5% or 30% plus cess is deducted.
  • A GIFT City fund for non-residents only: your gains are exempt from Indian income tax for non-resident investors (Schedule VI). The fund's own 10% tax on Indian income is taken to be inside the return.
  • An Indian international fund is taxed as other: 12.5% plus cess long-term, your slab rate plus cess short-term. For an NRI, 12.5% or 30% plus cess is deducted when the units are redeemed.
  • Shares or ETFs held directly through the LRS grow without payouts and are taxed at 12.5% plus cess long-term or your slab rate short-term. US tax on the gain is nil for someone who does not live in the US.
  • An Indian mutual fund through NRE or NRO: equity-oriented, 12.5% plus cess on gains above ₹1,25,000 after 12 months and 20% plus cess within; other, 12.5% after 24 months, and within 24 months 30% deducted with the final tax at your slab rate; debt (specified), 30% deducted and the final tax at your slab rate. The table counts the final tax; tax deducted beyond it comes back through your return.
  • NRE and FCNR(B) deposits are bank deposits, not mutual funds. Their interest is exempt from Indian income tax while you are a non-resident. NRE interest is added every quarter; FCNR(B) interest every 180 days on a 360-day year.

For a resident, the bank collects TCS of 20% on the part of the year's LRS remittances above ₹10,00,000. It is credited against your tax, so it is shown but not counted as a cost.

What the Calculator Assumes

Some points of law are not settled. The calculator takes one view of each and says so:

  • The GIFT City fund's own rates are those its offer document gives, 14.95% and 42.744%. One fund house notes the short-term rate could be 39% instead.
  • Whether your exit from a fund that takes residents is taxed again is not settled, so it is a switch.
  • No tax treaty relief is counted. Residents of the UAE and Singapore may be able to claim relief under their treaties; whether fund houses apply it when they deduct tax is not settled.
  • An NRI's basic exemption is not set against these gains: whether it may be is not settled.
  • TCS is on the part above ₹10,00,000 only, as banks collect it; the Act's wording does not settle it.
  • Gains on dollar holdings are changed into rupees at the start and end rates; how the Act does this is not settled.
  • An Indian international fund is taxed as other, not as a debt fund.
  • Growth only, with no payouts; other income under ₹50 lakh, so no surcharge; the new tax regime; and this is your only LRS remittance this year.

Worked Example

₹15,00,000 sent through the LRS for 5 years, at an assumed 12% a year with the rupee unchanged at ₹95.99 per dollar. The bank collects ₹1,00,000 of TCS, credited against your tax. The GIFT City fund, costing 1.11% a year, is worth ₹25,15,088 before tax; it pays ₹1,51,756 of tax on its own gains, which leaves ₹23,63,332 (USD 24,621). The Indian international fund, at 2.11%, ends at ₹24,03,710, less ₹1,17,482 of tax at 12.5% plus cess: ₹22,86,228. Shares held directly, at 0.25%, leave ₹24,69,302 after the same tax. If your exit from the GIFT City fund is taxed as well, it leaves ₹22,51,099.

Why Real Results Differ

  • Markets do not grow at a steady rate: returns change every year and can be negative, and the order of good and bad years changes the outcome.
  • Exchange rates move every day. The calculator uses today's rate and one yearly change.
  • Exit loads, brokerage, and the bank's currency spread and charges are not counted on any route.
  • Tax law and the way it applies to GIFT City funds can change, and several points are not settled.
  • Your country of residence may tax these gains too, and a tax treaty may change the Indian tax.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does the GIFT City fund show tax before I sell?

A GIFT City fund that takes residents is taxed as a trust. It provides for tax on its own gains and publishes its NAV after that tax, so the value you redeem at is already after the fund's tax.

Are an NRI's gains on a GIFT City fund exempt?

Only in a fund that non-residents alone may hold (Schedule VI). A fund that also takes residents is taxed for an NRI like any other fund, and its own tax is inside its NAV.

Why is TCS not counted as a cost?

TCS is tax collected in advance. It shows in your annual tax statement and is set off against your tax for the year, or refunded through your return. Until then, the money is tied up.

Can I check a particular fund?

Enter its yearly cost in the GIFT City fund's cost field. The default is the middle figure of the funds open to you, in the class sold through distributors; each fund's own figure is in its documents.

Why does every route have the same return?

To show the effect of tax, cost and currency alone. Real funds earn different returns from each other, and the rate you enter is an assumption, not any fund's return.
