NCD Calculator

A non-convertible debenture (NCD) pays interest at the coupon rate the issuer states for each series, monthly, quarterly, yearly or all at maturity, and repays the principal at the end. Enter a series' terms to see every payment with its date. Issue pages on this site link here with their series already filled in.

Usually a minimum of ₹10,000 (10 NCDs of ₹1,000), then in multiples of ₹1,000.

The issuer's stated rate for the series, from the offer document. For a cumulative series, enter its effective yield. Limited to 2% to 13%, like every calculator on this site.

Interest runs from this date. Each issue page shows it.

How the NCD Calculator Works

For a series that pays interest, each payment covers the days since the last one, on the Actual/Actual basis SEBI prescribes for debt securities: a period that includes 29 February is divided by 366, any other by 365.

Interestk = P × c × dk ÷ 365 (366 if the period includes 29 Feb)

where P is the amount, c the coupon rate as a decimal and dk the days in period k. The principal is repaid with the last payment. A cumulative series compounds once a year and pays everything at maturity:

Maturity amount = P × (1 + y)months ÷ 12

where y is the series' effective yield. The effective yield shown is the XIRR of all the payments, the same measure as the XIRR calculator, so series with different payout options can be compared.

Worked Example

₹1,00,000 in an annual-payout series with a 9.5% coupon, allotted on 5 Oct 2026, for 36 months. Each year's interest is ₹1,00,000 × 9.5% × days ÷ days in the year. The year to 5 Oct 2028 has 366 days and includes 29 Feb 2028, so it is divided by 366: ₹9,500, the same as in the 365-day years. Three payments of ₹9,500 add up to ₹28,500, and the last one also returns the ₹1,00,000. A monthly series of the same issue at 9.11% pays between ₹699 and ₹774 a month, depending on the days in the month, ₹27,353 in all, for an effective yield of 9.50%. A cumulative series at 9.5% pays nothing until maturity and then ₹1,31,293.

Why Real Results Differ

  • The issuer may delay or fail to pay interest or principal; the calculator assumes every payment is made on time.
  • The offer document's cash-flow table is the reference: payment dates that fall on holidays, record dates and rounding can move a payment by a day or a rupee.
  • TDS of 10% is deducted from interest on listed NCDs above ₹10,000 a year, and interest is taxed at your slab rate.
  • If you sell on the exchange before maturity, you receive the market price, which can be above or below the face value.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are monthly payments not all the same?

Interest is worked out on the actual days in each period, so a 31-day month pays a little more than a 30-day month, and February less.

What is the effective yield?

The yearly return if you hold to maturity, taking into account how often interest is paid. A monthly coupon is set slightly lower than an annual one so that both give a similar effective yield.

Why is the coupon limited to 13%?

Every calculator on this site keeps rates between 2% and 13%. Coupons of NCD public issues are usually within that range; the offer document states the exact rate.

How is NCD interest taxed?

At your slab rate, as income from other sources, with TDS of 10% on listed NCDs above ₹10,000 a year. The NCD tax guide explains capital gains if you sell before maturity.
