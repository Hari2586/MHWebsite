Capital Gain Bond Calculator

Capital Gain Bonds save the tax on a long-term capital gain from land or buildings, if you invest the gain within six months of the sale. This calculator works out the tax saved on a long-term capital gain from land or buildings, and the interest on Capital Gain Bonds at the issuer's current rate and dates. It is an illustration: the issuer's Information Memorandum is the reference, and every payment depends on the issuer paying on time. These are tax-specific bonds, meant for investors claiming the Section 85 exemption on long-term capital gains from land or buildings, subject to its conditions. Capital Gain Bonds are not bank deposits, are not insured by DICGC and are not guaranteed by the Government of India. They carry the issuer's credit risk, and ratings can change. They are locked in for 5 years, cannot be transferred, pledged or sold, and are not listed. The interest is taxable. The exemption depends on meeting the conditions of Section 85, and tax laws can change. The issuer may revise the coupon or close the issue early. Consult a tax adviser. Based on income-tax rules in force on 29 Sep 2026. This is general information, not tax advice. Please consult a tax professional.

How the Capital Gain Bond Calculator Works

The gain you invest, up to the gain itself and ₹50,00,000, is not taxed. Long-term gains on land and buildings are taxed at 12.5%, plus surcharge if your income calls for it and 4% cess, so:

Tax saved = min(investment, gain) × 12.5% × (1 + surcharge) × 1.04

Interest runs from the day the issuer receives your money. The bonds are allotted on the last day of that month and repaid five years later. Interest is paid once a year on the issuer's date: 30 June for REC, 31 July for PFC and 15 October for IRFC. Money received by the issuer's cutoff (31 May for REC and PFC, 31 August for IRFC) earns its first interest that year; later money earns it the next year, for a longer first period. A full year pays the coupon; a part-year pays:

Interest = amount × coupon × days ÷ 365 (366 if the period includes 29 Feb)

rounded to the rupee. The last payment covers the part-year up to maturity.

Worked Example

A flat bought in 2015 is sold on 20 Aug 2026 with a long-term capital gain of ₹30,00,000. The whole gain is invested in REC Series XX at 5.25%, and REC receives the money on 15 Oct 2026, well before the last date, 20 Feb 2027. The gain is not taxed: at 12.5% plus 4% cess that saves ₹3,90,000. The bonds pay ₹1,11,329 for the part-year to 30 Jun 2027, then ₹1,57,500 each year, and a last part-year payment of ₹53,075 with the ₹30,00,000 on 31 Oct 2031: ₹7,94,404 of interest in all, or ₹5,46,550 after tax at the 30% slab plus cess.

Why Real Results Differ

  • The exemption depends on meeting every condition of Section 85: a long-term gain on land or buildings, invested within six months, and kept for five years.
  • The ₹50,00,000 limit is for the tax year of the sale and the next year together, including any earlier investment in these bonds.
  • Payment dates that fall on holidays, and the issuer's own rounding, can move a payment by a day or a rupee.
  • Surcharge on the interest, set-offs of losses and the choice of indexation for land bought before 23 Jul 2024 are not included.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I invest the whole sale price?

You can invest up to ₹50,00,000, but only the part up to the capital gain saves tax. The rest earns interest only.

Why is the first interest payment different?

Interest runs from the day the issuer receives your money to its first interest date, so the first period is shorter or longer than a year.

Is the interest taxable?

Yes, at your slab rate. The issuers deduct no tax at source for resident investors, so it is declared in your return; non-residents have tax deducted.

Which date counts for the six months?

Treat the date the issuer receives your money as the investment date, and invest well before the six months end.
