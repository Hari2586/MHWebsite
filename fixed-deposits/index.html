Fixed Deposits

Company and bank FDs we offer, with the rates for your investor type

See the 8 FDs We Offer

A fixed deposit (FD) is money you place with a bank or a company for a fixed term, at an interest rate fixed on the day you invest. You choose how long to invest and whether to take the interest regularly or at the end. MoneyHoney offers FDs from 8 deposit takers: 2 banks, 3 housing finance companies and 3 non-banking financial companies (NBFCs). Bank FDs are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank; company FDs are not insured and carry a credit rating.

Each company's rates carry its own w.e.f. date. Rules as on 3 Oct 2026.

How a Fixed Deposit Works

  1. 1

    Choose Who Is Investing

    Who can invest, and at what rate, depends on the investor: an individual, NRI, HUF, business or trust.

  2. 2

    Pick a Company, Tenure and Payout

    Cumulative adds the interest to the deposit and pays it at maturity; non-cumulative pays it monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly.

  3. 3

    Invest on the Investor Platform

    Sign in to MoneyHoney's investor platform to complete KYC and pay. Rates can change. The rate on the day you invest applies.

  4. 4

    Get Your Money Back at Maturity

    The deposit and any unpaid interest are paid to your bank account, unless you renew. Early withdrawal pays a lower rate.

Example

You put ₹1,00,000 in LIC Housing Finance's Sanchay deposit for 3 years (rates w.e.f. 1 Sep 2026). With the cumulative option, interest of 7.20% p.a. is added each year, and you get ₹1,23,193 at maturity before tax: ₹23,193 of interest, an effective yield of 7.73% a year. With monthly payout the rate is 6.95% p.a.: you get ₹579 a month and your ₹1,00,000 back at the end. Try your own figures in the FD Calculator.

Bank FD or Company FD

Bank FDs and company FDs differ. Bank deposits are insured by DICGC up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank; company deposits are not insured and depend on the company's ability to repay. Ratings, lock-in, early-withdrawal terms and TDS thresholds also differ. Compare these, not only the rate.

Words You Will See on an FD Page

Interest Rate
The rate the deposit earns, in % p.a. It is fixed for the term.
Effective Yield
Effective yield is the average yearly gain on a cumulative deposit, counting compounding, before tax.
Cumulative
Interest is added to the deposit and paid with it at maturity.
Non-Cumulative
Interest is paid out monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or yearly, at a slightly lower rate for more frequent payouts.
Rates w.e.f.
The date the company's current rate card took effect.
DICGC Cover
Deposit insurance for banks: up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank. Company FDs do not have it.
Credit Rating
An agency's opinion of a company's ability to repay, such as Crisil AAA/Stable. It can change.
Premature Withdrawal
Closing an FD before maturity. Companies lock deposits in for 3 months; after that, and at banks, a lower rate applies.
Form 121
The declaration, which replaced Forms 15G and 15H, that stops tax being deducted when your tax is nil.

Individuals, NRIs, HUFs, businesses, trusts and some institutions. Which FDs are open, and the rate, depend on the investor. FDs open to each, of the 8 we offer:

Understand the Risks

  • Company FDs are not insured. Repayment depends on the company. Its credit rating is an opinion, and it can change.
  • Early exit costs. Company FDs are locked in for 3 months; after that, and at banks, early withdrawal pays a lower rate.
  • The rate is fixed. If rates rise you keep the lower rate, and when the FD matures you may reinvest at a lower one.
  • Interest is taxable. It is taxed at your slab rate, with TDS above the threshold, and tax laws can change.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an FD with a company insured like a bank FD?

No. DICGC insures deposits with banks, up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank. Deposits with NBFCs and housing finance companies are not insured; repayment depends on the company. Check its credit rating, and remember ratings can change.

Can I withdraw an FD early?

Yes, at a cost. Banks pay the rate for the period the deposit ran, less a penalty. Company FDs are locked in for 3 months except on death or in an emergency. After that, NBFCs pay no interest before 6 months and 2% below the rate for the period run after it; housing finance companies pay up to 4% a year to individuals between 3 and 6 months, and 1% below the rate after it.

How is FD interest taxed?

Interest is taxable. Tax is deducted at 10% once interest from the company exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year (20% without PAN). If your tax is nil, you can submit Form 121, which replaced Forms 15G and 15H. At a bank, the threshold is ₹50,000 (₹1,00,000 for senior citizens).

Can NRIs invest in these FDs?

Yes, at 7 of the 8. NRIs can invest only from an NRO account, on a non-repatriation basis, for 12 to 36 months. HDFC Bank takes NRE and NRO deposits. For NRIs, tax is deducted at 30% plus cess on NRO and company FD interest; NRE interest is tax-free. NRIs cannot submit Form 121.

Can a trust invest?

Deposits with banks and housing finance companies are a permitted investment for charitable and religious trusts under section 350 of the Income-tax Act, 2025, read with Schedule XVI. Deposits with Bajaj Finance, Shriram Finance and Mahindra Finance are not; other trusts should check their trust deed.

What is the difference between the interest rate and the effective yield?

The interest rate is the yearly rate on the deposit. On a cumulative FD, interest earns interest, so the average yearly gain is higher: 7.20% p.a. compounded yearly for 3 years is an effective yield of 7.73%. Both are before tax.

A fixed deposit with an NBFC or a housing finance company is an unsecured deposit. It is not a bank deposit and is not insured by DICGC. Interest and repayment depend on the company's ability to pay. Its rating is the rating agency's opinion and can change. Money is locked in for the first 3 months except in limited cases, and early withdrawal after that pays a lower rate.

A bank FD is insured by DICGC up to ₹5,00,000 per depositor per bank, principal and interest together. Early withdrawal pays the rate for the period the deposit ran, less a penalty.

NBFCs: The Reserve Bank of India does not accept any responsibility or guarantee about the financial soundness of the company, the correctness of its statements, or the repayment of deposits.

Housing finance companies: The Reserve Bank of India and the National Housing Bank do not accept any responsibility or guarantee about the financial soundness of the company, the correctness of its statements, or the repayment of deposits.
